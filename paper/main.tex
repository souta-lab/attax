\title{Attax: Guideline-Versus Strength from a 24-Parameter Beam Search,
and a World-Model Failure under the S2 Ruleset}
\author{souta-lab (s0u7a)}
\date{Draft v2 --- November 2026}

\maketitle
\begin{abstract}
We present Attax, a versus bot for the falling-block game built on a
single beam search over the known piece queue, scored by a linear
evaluator with eleven board features and 24 parameters in total. Under
guideline versus rules, Attax defeats every opponent it has been
measured against: a byte-faithful replica of the Hoiko binary (195--5
over 200 held-out games), the Monte Carlo beam-search bot clys (88--12),
the official Cold Clear 2 engine (79--21), and a Cold Clear 2 fork
(27--13) --- in the Cold Clear 2 match while generating \emph{less}
attack per piece than its opponent (0.8951 against 0.9279), winning on
survival rather than firepower. We treat the evaluation protocol ---
fixed seed partitions, held-out headline seeds, pre-registered pass bars
and abort rules, no re-rolls --- as a contribution in its own right. We
then report a case study in which the same bot, taken to the S2 ruleset,
lost its first counted match 0--20 to the reigning lab champion, Sold
Slear, and two pre-registered SPSA weight-tuning campaigns failed to
produce measurable improvement: the first ended with its selection
procedure retaining the untuned seed profile, the second was stopped by
its own registered abort rule. A subsequent independent audit explains
why: the campaigns had been tuning weights for a search whose internal
world model diverged from the referee on the death boundary, on garbage
materialization, and on spin evidence. The negative result is the point
--- under a fixed protocol, it is attributable to specific, verified
defects rather than to tuning effort.
\end{abstract}

\section{1. Introduction}\label{introduction}

Strong play in versus falling-block games has historically come from two
directions: large searches with hand-crafted linear evaluators, and, in
the single-player setting, approximate dynamic programming over compact
feature sets. The published evaluation reference point remains CBMPI
(Gabillon et al., 2013), which reached roughly 51 million lines on the
mdptetris protocol with a linear evaluator over nine hand-crafted
features. The versus game adds an opponent, garbage exchange, and
ruleset-specific attack economics, and it is there that widely used
engines such as Cold Clear 2 set the practical standard.

Attax is an attempt to see how far a deliberately small design goes: one
beam search over the whole known queue as the only decision core, one
weight table of 24 parameters, and the game's award rules implemented
once, in a core engine that both the referee and the search consult.
Section 2 describes the architecture. Section 3 describes the evaluation
protocol, which we regard as part of the contribution: every headline
number in Section 4 is produced by a fixed pipeline from checked-in game
records, tuning and headline seed sets are disjoint by construction, and
bars are registered before runs, not after.

Section 5 is a case study of failure. Ported to the S2 ruleset --- a
different attack economy with all-spin awards, a surge mechanic, and
garbage that tanks on a delay --- Attax lost heavily to the lab champion
Sold Slear, and two weight-tuning campaigns designed and registered in
advance failed to move it. Because the campaigns were run under the same
protocol discipline, their failure could be audited rather than merely
regretted: an independent verification pass over the S2 driver confirmed
a list of concrete defects in the search's model of the game, each
sufficient to corrupt the tuning signal. We report the defects, the
campaign outcomes, and the follow-on programme (driver correction,
mechanism ablation, and two mechanism ports) as ongoing work. No S2
strength result is claimed.

\section{2. Architecture}\label{architecture}

Attax is a from-scratch implementation in TypeScript, with the search
hot path also ported to Rust. Its structure follows one rule: the rules
engine (\texttt{src/core}) is the single source of truth for what a lock
awards, and the bot's search prices lines using exactly those awards.

\textbf{Move generation.} Placements are generated by breadth-first
search over the engine's own inputs --- shifts, soft drop, and rotations
with first-fit SRS kicks --- over a bitboard representation. Every
emitted placement is therefore reachable by construction, a property the
test suite checks exhaustively against real engine inputs. Spin
classification uses the engine's own function, so the search cannot
disagree with the referee about what a spin is.

\textbf{Evaluation.} Let \(s\) be a board with column heights
\(h_1, \dots, h_{10}\), \(H = \max_x h_x\), and let \(L \ge 0\) be the
number of garbage rows that have landed under the stack along the line
leading to \(s\). The board evaluator is linear in eleven features
\(\varphi(s, L)\):

\begin{equation}f(s) = \mathbf{w} \cdot \varphi(s, L) = \sum_{i=1}^{11} w_i \, \varphi_i(s, L).
\end{equation}

The features are: \emph{maxHeight} \(= H + L\); \emph{danger}
\(= \max(0, H +
L - 10)^2\); \emph{holes}, the empty cells with a filled cell above them
in their column; \emph{holeCover}, the filled cells stacked above holes,
capped at 4 per hole; \emph{holeRows}, the rows containing at least one
hole; \emph{bumpiness} \(= \sum_x |h_x - h_{x+1}|\) and
\emph{bumpinessSq} \(= \sum_x (h_x - h_{x+1})^2\), both sums excluding
the pairs adjacent to the well column (the argmin of \(h_x\));
\emph{rowTransitions}, the filled/empty transitions along each row with
the walls counted as filled; \emph{wellDepth}
\(= \min(4, \min(h_{w-1}, h_{w+1})
- h_w)\) for well column \(w\); \emph{extraWells}, the columns other
than \(w\) sitting at least 3 below both neighbours; and \emph{tSlot}
\(\in \{0, 1, 2\}\), the number of lines a T-spin into the best open
T-slot would clear.

\textbf{Attack.} The rules engine prices a lock that clears \(\ell\)
lines with spin class
\(\sigma \in \{\text{none}, \text{mini}, \text{full}\}\), post-lock
combo counter \(c\), incoming back-to-back state \(b\), and
perfect-clear flag \(\mathrm{PC}\) as

\begin{equation}A = B(\ell, \sigma) + \mathbf{1}[\,b \wedge \mathrm{difficult}(\ell, \sigma)\,] + \beta(c) + 10 \cdot \mathbf{1}[\mathrm{PC}],
\end{equation}

where \(\mathrm{difficult}(\ell, \sigma) \Leftrightarrow \ell > 0 \wedge
(\ell = 4 \vee \sigma \neq \text{none})\), the base table \(B\) (tuples
indexed by \(\ell\) from 0) is

\begin{equation}B(\ell, \sigma) = \begin{cases} (0, 0, 1, 2, 4)_\ell & \sigma = \text{none} \\ (0, 2, 4, 6)_\ell & \sigma = \text{full} \\ (0, 0, 1, 0)_\ell & \sigma = \text{mini} \end{cases}
\end{equation}

(the full T-spin row is indexed by T-spin lines, the mini row by lines
cleared), and the combo bonus is the step function

\begin{equation}\beta(c) = \begin{cases} 0 & c \le 0 \\ 1 & 1 \le c \le 2 \\ 2 & 3 \le c \le 4 \\ 3 & 5 \le c \le 6 \\ 4 & 7 \le c \le 9 \\ 5 & c \ge 10. \end{cases}
\end{equation}

The state updates are \(c \leftarrow c + 1\) on a clear and
\(c \leftarrow -1\) otherwise, and \(b\) persists through non-clears and
on a clear becomes \(\mathrm{difficult}(\ell, \sigma)\) --- or
\(\ell \ge 4\) after a perfect clear. An attack first cancels the
attacker's own pending garbage; the remainder is queued to the opponent
and lands after a one-lock delay. Inside the search only the root's
pending amount \(P\) is known, and what lands enters the state as lift,
\(L \leftarrow L + \max(0, P - A)\) at the root lock.

\textbf{Reward.} Each lock on a searched line earns

\begin{equation}r = w_{\mathrm{atk}} \, A + c_{\mathrm{kind}} + w_{\mathrm{brk}} \cdot \mathbf{1}[\,b \wedge \ell > 0 \wedge \neg\mathrm{difficult} \wedge \neg\mathrm{PC}\,] + w_{\mathrm{wst}} \cdot \mathbf{1}[\,\text{T locked without a spin} \wedge \varphi_{\mathrm{tSlot}}(s) > 0\,],
\end{equation}

where \(A\) is the attack of (2), priced before cancellation,
\(c_{\mathrm{kind}}\) is the shaping value of the lock's clear kind
(single through quad, T-spin mini/single/double/triple, perfect clear;
zero when \(\ell = 0\)), and the last term reads the T-slot feature of
the board \emph{before} the lock. The complete policy is one table of 24
parameters:

\textbf{Search.} A node at depth \(D\) on a line whose locks earned
\(r_1, \dots,
r_D\) and ended on board \(s_D\) is valued

\begin{equation}V(n) = \sum_{i=1}^{D} \gamma^{i-1} r_i + \gamma^D f(s_D),
\end{equation}

with \(\gamma = 0.97\). At each ply the beam expands every surviving
node with every reachable placement of the current piece and of the hold
piece, discards children whose post-lock stack reaches the top row, and
keeps

\begin{equation}\mathcal{B}_{d+1} = \text{the } W \text{ highest-}V \text{ children, deduplicated on } (\text{queue index}, \text{hold}, b, \text{board}),
\end{equation}

sorting before deduplication so the higher-\(V\) copy of a position
survives. Lines end when the known queue (the current piece, the hold
piece, and the five-piece preview) is exhausted; the first lock of the
line ending at the highest-\(V\) finished node is played --- or, if
every line tops out, of the deepest surviving beam. The default width is
\(W = 48\).

In the profile used for the external matches of Section 4 (the
\emph{field-40} profile), the search board is 40 rows tall and the known
queue is extended by one exact chance ply --- SPECULATE. Let
\(\mathcal{R}\) be the set of piece kinds not yet observed in the
current 7-bag, tracked exactly (if the bag boundary cannot be
established from the observed queue, \(\mathcal{R} =
\emptyset\) and the ply is skipped). The bag holds one of each kind, so
each \(k \in \mathcal{R}\) is equally likely, and a horizon node \(n\)
is scored

\begin{equation}V(n) = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{R}|} \sum_{k \in \mathcal{R}} \max_{a \in \mathcal{A}(n, k)} V(\mathrm{child}(n, a; k)),
\end{equation}

where \(\mathcal{A}(n, k)\) ranges over the placements of kind \(k\)
(with the hold option) and child values are (6) extended by one lock. A
kind with no legal placement contributes \(-10^6\) rather than being
dropped, so a dead branch cannot vanish from the chance average.

\textbf{Rust port.} The search was ported to Rust for throughput. On a
sampled corpus of 298 recorded decision inputs, each evaluated with and
without the chance ply (596 cases), the port's decisions agreed with the
TypeScript reference in 596 of 596 cases; residual differences were
confined to floating-point leaf values (largest absolute difference \(\approx\)
2.3 \(\times\) 10\textsuperscript{-13}, traced to a one-ulp difference in the power function at the
chance depth).\textsuperscript{1}

\section{3. Evaluation protocol}\label{evaluation-protocol}

The protocol is fixed by the repository's working rules and is the same
for every result in Section 4.

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Seed partitions.} Tuning uses seeds 2001 and above only. Each
  headline matchup uses its own fresh partition, disjoint from tuning
  seeds and from every other matchup's partition.
\item
  \textbf{Both seats.} Every seed is played from both seats. Where
  seat-swapped replays are bit-identical (the Hoiko harness), each seed
  is counted once and the identity of the replays is itself reported.
\item
  \textbf{Records first.} Scores exist only as generated aggregates of
  checked-in per-game records (\texttt{bench-results/*.jsonl}, produced
  by \texttt{scripts/build-results.ts}). No number is transcribed by
  hand. External matches were imported as per-seed records only when
  their replay verification passed, and the generator asserts the
  imported summaries against the records.
\item
  \textbf{Bars before runs.} Pass bars, abort thresholds, and validation
  seed sets for tuning campaigns are registered in writing before the
  run starts (Section 5). A missed bar is reported as a miss; a stopped
  campaign is not restarted on fresh seeds to try again.
\item
  \textbf{Uncertainty.} Win rates carry 95\% Wilson score intervals over
  decided games (\(n = W + L\), \(\hat p = W / n\), \(z = 1.96\)):
\end{itemize}

\begin{equation}\frac{\hat p + z^2 / 2n \;\mp\; z \sqrt{\hat p (1 - \hat p) / n + z^2 / 4n^2}}{1 + z^2 / n}
\end{equation}

For external matches the intervals are descriptive: the independent
units are seed pairs, not individual games. - \textbf{Attack rate.} We
report attack per piece (APP) alongside wins. The two harnesses define
it differently --- the Hoiko harness counts garbage \emph{sent after
cancellation} per piece; the external rig counts attack \emph{generated
before cancellation} per piece --- and figures are only compared within
a harness.

Two opponent-specific notes. Hoiko is measured through a Rust/WASM
replica of the shipping binary, because the benchmark machine cannot run
the original executable; the replica agreed with the executable on 900
of 900 moves across three validation seeds (500, 200, and 200 moves),
and the rules audit shows the engine awarding exactly the clears Hoiko
predicts on 843 of 843 locks. This is strong evidence about the replica,
not a proof of equivalence, and we flag it again in Section 7. Cold
Clear 2 and its fork were played over the Tetris Bot Protocol (TBP): the
engine received a 100 ms window per move at 5 PPS paired turns, with the
engine process suspended while Attax searched, so both sides received
the same wall-clock window.

\section{5. Case study: the S2
campaign}\label{case-study-the-s2-campaign}

\subsection{5.1 Arena and champion}\label{arena-and-champion}

The S2 ruleset --- the versus rules of TETR.IO's second season, with
all-spin awards, back-to-back charging, a surge release, and garbage
that tanks after a delay --- is available to us only through the
\emph{s2-bot-lab} of 61bi, an independent re-implementation built from
observation of the live game. It is not the official referee, and
everything in this section is conditioned on that (Section 7). The lab's
reigning champion is Sold Slear 1.4, left at its lab defaults and
unweakened throughout. Source analysis (the champion's WASM rebuilds
byte-identical from the public tree, so the analysis is of the deployed
code, not an approximation) shows the champion to be the Cold Clear 2
engine plus an added layer, F14: a leaf-level conversion shaper that
prices the conversion of combo (REN) stock into back-to-back and surge
pressure --- with a scale of \(\approx\) 0.116 per conversion unit, a penalty of
\(-\)0.6 on shallow REN, and the shaping switched off above board height 8
--- together with a quantity-only solvency veto at the root (a margin
\(M = 20 - h - T -
I\) over height \(h\), tanked garbage \(T\), and remaining input \(I\))
and a top-out fallback. Its weights are the eleventh of eleven published
SPSA generations; generation comments indicate the seventh generation
was tuned for attack rate and the tenth and eleventh for attack rate
plus wins. A widely repeated scoreline for the champion, 236--20 against
the Cold Clear 2 fork, appears nowhere in the public source; it is
self-reported from a private staging repository, and we do not rely on
it.\textsuperscript{2}

Attax's legal information in the lab is its own board and garbage
amounts only, and the chance ply is disabled in the lab driver (the
lab's queues are not 7-bag). Its first counted match, on 2026-11-14
(seeds 3101--3120), was a 0--20 loss, with attack rate 0.8520 against
the champion's 1.1373.\textsuperscript{3}

\subsection{5.2 Two tuning campaigns}\label{two-tuning-campaigns}

Both campaigns tuned the S2 profile's weights --- partitioned into
\(G = 11\) coupled groups --- by maximizing, over blocks of \(n\) games,

\begin{equation}J(\theta) = \frac{W - L}{n} + \overline{\mathrm{APP}}_{\mathrm{attax}} - \overline{\mathrm{APP}}_{\mathrm{opp}} + \lambda_s \cdot \frac{1}{n} \sum_{g=1}^{n} \log_2(\mathrm{locks}_g + 1),
\end{equation}

where \(\overline{\mathrm{APP}}\) is the mean over games of per-game
attack per lock and \(\mathrm{locks}_g\) is Attax's lock count in game
\(g\). The optimizer is SPSA on the groups: at iteration
\(k = 0, 1, 2, \dots\), with a Rademacher perturbation vector
\(\Delta_k \in \{-1, +1\}^G\) (frozen groups held at 0),

\begin{equation}\theta^{\pm} = \theta_k \pm c_k \Delta_k, \qquad \hat g_k = \frac{J(\theta^{+}) - J(\theta^{-})}{2 c_k}, \qquad \theta_{k+1} = \Pi\big[\theta_k + a_k \, \hat g_k \, \Delta_k\big],
\end{equation}

applied componentwise to every parameter of each group, in per-parameter
normalized units, and projected (\(\Pi\)) onto registered bounds, with
gain sequences

\begin{equation}a_k = \frac{0.3}{(k + 11)^{0.602}}, \qquad c_k = \frac{0.12}{(k + 1)^{0.101}}.
\end{equation}

\textbf{Campaign 1 (league SPSA).} After a preliminary fixed-target SPSA
phase whose gradients an independent audit found to be noise-dominated,
the main campaign tuned against a pool of the frozen champion (\(\approx\) 50\%)
and Attax snapshots (\(\approx\) 50\%), under (10) with \(\lambda_s = 0\) and
\(n_{\mathrm{arm}} =
4\) games per evaluation arm. The pre-registered bar for the held-out
validation (40 games, fresh seeds) was at least 8 wins and a selection
score above the seed profile's. The campaign missed it on both
checkpoints: the intermediate validation scored 0--40, and the final
validation 3--36 with one game excluded (APP 0.7544 against 1.0765). The
final selection procedure, given the seed profile, the final iterate,
and the best-evaluated iterate, retained the seed profile itself. An
audit of the objective (audit item J1) confirmed saturation on the
champion axis: against an opponent this much stronger, the win term is
almost always saturated and the attack-rate term is dominated by the
opponent's own scoring, so the objective carries little gradient about
Attax's play.\textsuperscript{4}

\textbf{Campaign 2 (redesigned objective, registered abort).} The second
campaign tuned against the champion only, with the objective redesigned
to repair the saturation: \(\lambda_s = 0.3\) in (10); the perfect-clear
weight group was frozen (\(\Delta_i = 0\) in (11)), and
\(n_{\mathrm{arm}}\) was raised to 8. Its design pre-registered
early-abort probes at iterations 30, 60, and 90, as the formal criterion

\begin{equation}\text{abort} \iff \bar{\Delta J} \le +0.10,
\end{equation}

where \(\bar{\Delta J}\) is the mean paired per-game difference
\(J(\theta_k)
- J(\theta_{\mathrm{seed}})\) over the probe block. The first stepped
iterations showed the shaping term doing what it was designed to do ---
J values of +0.45 and +0.64 on arms that nevertheless lost all eight
games --- but at the iteration-30 probe (40 paired games, no exclusions)
\(\bar{\Delta J}\) measured +0.0834 with standard deviation 0.581: a
positive point estimate well inside noise. The campaign aborted under
its own rule.\textsuperscript{5} A later audit of the probe found two accounting defects
worth recording here: only 21 of the 30 counted iterations had produced
successful updates (the rest were transport or asymmetric exclusions),
and the probe's seat layout deviated from its registration (40 distinct
seeds played once, alternating seats, rather than 20 seeds in both
seats). The comparison remained paired on seed and seat and the abort
decision stands, but the episode is itself evidence for Section 3's
point: registrations must be implemented exactly, and audited.\textsuperscript{6}

\subsection{5.3 The world-model audit}\label{the-world-model-audit}

Two failed campaigns admit two readings: the weights cannot express the
needed play, or the thing being tuned is not the game being played. An
independent verification pass over the S2 driver source --- conducted
after both campaigns, against the referee harness line by line ---
established the second reading. The confirmed defects:\textsuperscript{7}

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Death boundary.} The search discarded a line only when the
  stack filled the full 40-row board; the referee declares a player dead
  when any cell occupies the 20 rows above the visible field. The search
  therefore retained, expanded, and could select lines whose post-lock
  states the referee had already ruled dead --- and move generation
  emitted placements locking inside that buffer zone at every depth.
\item
  \textbf{Garbage never lands.} Pending garbage was applied only at the
  root, and even there only as a scalar added to a height feature. Child
  boards never received the garbage rows the referee would have tanked,
  so every placement beyond depth 0 was planned into bottom rows that,
  in the real future, are occupied.
\item
  \textbf{Garbage bookkeeping.} After a line clear, the per-row garbage
  flags were compacted in the opposite direction to the board rows
  themselves, misaligning every surviving flag by one row per cleared
  row; deeper garbage-clear bonuses were priced on garbage that was not
  there.
\item
  \textbf{Spin evidence erased.} Move generation zeroed the rotation
  evidence of every non-T placement, and the S2 spin classifier
  short-circuits on exactly that zero. Non-T spins were consequently
  never priced anywhere in the search, and back-to-back continuation
  through non-T spins was never valued --- in a ruleset whose attack
  economy runs on all-spin play.
\item
  \textbf{State merging.} The beam's deduplication key omitted
  landed-garbage amount and garbage provenance, merging nodes whose
  futures differ.
\item
  \textbf{A weaker conversion term than believed.} The root conversion
  reward --- the driver's only term that could price stock --- was
  root-only and, at the saved profiles' scales, worth single-digit
  points against attack rewards in the hundreds; in the seed profile it
  was exactly zero, and campaign 2's tuning drove it negative.
\item
  \textbf{A mismeasured baseline.} The S2 driver had run every campaign
  game at beam width 48, inherited from the bot defaults, while campaign
  documents assumed 96.
\end{enumerate}

Both campaigns, in other words, tuned weights for a search whose
imagined futures diverged from the referee on death, garbage, and spins
simultaneously. SPSA was faithfully optimizing the wrong game. This also
reframes campaign 1's audit finding: objective saturation was real, but
it was measured on top of a transition model that was not the referee's.

\subsection{5.4 Follow-on programme (ongoing
work)}\label{follow-on-programme-ongoing-work}

The response, in registered order, is: correct the driver defects with
the guideline path fenced off and its regression suite as the
non-interference proof; ablate the champion's mechanisms (conversion
shaping off, solvency veto off, both off, and the unmodified Cold Clear
2 profile) to measure what each mechanism is worth before porting
anything; port a stock potential and a geometry-aware solvency rule;
then fit weights by imitation of the champion's move choices before any
further SPSA, with tuning gates sized so that the standard error of a
paired comparison is at most a third of the effect worth detecting.

The stock potential is a state potential added to the terminal bracket
at every beam layer --- (6) with the bracket extended:

\begin{equation}V_D = \sum_{i=1}^{D} \gamma^{i-1} r_i + \gamma^D \big[ f(s_D) + \Phi_{\mathrm{stock}}(s_D) \big],
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}\Phi_{\mathrm{stock}}(s) = w_A \Big[ q_B(s) \, S(d, m) + g(s) \big( \beta_B U_B(s) + \beta_R U_R(s) \big) \Big],
\end{equation}

where \(w_A\) is the attack weight (score units per attack row), \(d\)
is the difficult-clear chain count, \(S(d, m)\) is the cashable surge
under the lab's authoritative award function at current multiplier \(m\)
(zero at \(d
\le 4\)), \(q_B(s)\) is the estimated probability of converting the
cashable stock before death, \(U_B\) is the additional value of
preserving or building the chain, and \(U_R\) is the incremental future
attack attributable to the current REN stock:

\begin{equation}U_R(s) = \max_{\pi \in \mathcal{P}_K(s)} \sum_{j=1}^{K} \gamma^{j-1} \Big[ A_j(\pi \mid c) - A_j(\pi \mid c = -1) \Big],
\end{equation}

the difference between continuing a continuation \(\pi\) with the
current combo counter \(c\) and with the chain reset, over identical
moves (\(K = 3\) in the port). Only the growth terms are gated, by
\(g = g_h g_m\) with \(g_h
= \mathrm{clip}((14 - h)/6, 0, 1)\) on post-lock height \(h\) and
\(g_m =
\mathrm{clip}(M_K/6, 0, 1)\) on the estimated worst safety margin
\(M_K\); cashable surge is discounted by \(q_B\) but never
height-erased. The solvency port tracks residual pending through the
search, \(P_{j+1} = P_j -
C_j - T_j + I_j\) (cancellation \(C_j\), tank \(T_j\), arrivals
\(I_j\)), and scores a continuation under scenario \(\omega\) by its
worst margin \(M(\pi, \omega) = \min_j [20 - h_j(\pi, \omega)]\) ---
using the garbage geometry Attax can legally observe, where the
champion's veto (Section 5.1) sees only amounts.

As implemented, both mechanisms sit inside the bracket of (6):

\begin{equation}V(n) = \sum_{i=1}^{D} \gamma^{i-1} r_i + \gamma^D \big[ f(s_D) + \Phi(s_D) - \mathrm{risk}(s_D) \big],
\end{equation}

with \(\Phi\) as in (15) up to a profile scale factor and the
implementation's \(q_B = \mathrm{clip}((18 - (h + P))/8, 0, 1)\) on
effective height, and with
\(\mathrm{risk}(s) = 40 \cdot \max(0, 6 - M)^2 + 10^6 \cdot
\mathbf{1}[M < 0]\) on the exact post-lock margin \(M = 20 - H - P\).
One deliberate deviation from the canonical design: the design also
writes \(\Phi\) in potential-shaping transition form,
\(F(s, a, s') = \gamma \Phi(s')
- \Phi(s)\); the port instead pays \(\Phi\) once per node in the
terminal bracket, which preserves the never-pay-twice invariant the
transition form protects. Beam-slot reservation for both mechanisms is
deferred until telemetry shows stock-preserving lines disappearing
despite the potential.

The imitation fit, finally, minimizes the set-valued ranking loss

\begin{equation}L_{\mathrm{choice}} = -\sum_s w_s \log \frac{\sum_{a \in A^*_s} e^{Q_\theta(s, a) / \tau}}{\sum_{a \in A_s} e^{Q_\theta(s, a) / \tau}} + \lambda \, \| \theta - \theta_0 \|_2^2,
\end{equation}

where \(A_s\) is the set of legal root actions at \(s\),
\(A^*_s \subseteq A_s\) the teacher-preferred or empirically
near-equivalent actions, \(Q_\theta\) the search's action value under
weights \(\theta\), \(\tau\) a temperature, \(w_s\) a per-state weight,
and the second term anchors the fit to the seed weights \(\theta_0\); a
separate survival loss prices avoidable-death decisions, so a rare fatal
error is not outweighed by frequent safe ones. None of this has produced
an S2 result at the time of writing, and none is claimed.

\section{6. Discussion}\label{discussion}

\textbf{What transferred, and what did not.} The guideline results show
the core design --- reachability-exact move generation, one linear
evaluator, beam search over the known queue with an exact chance ply ---
is strong where its world model is the referee's world model: in the
home harness the rules engine \emph{is} the search's transition
function, and in the TBP matches the adapter's view matched the host's.
Under S2, three distinct things failed to transfer, and the campaign
record separates them. The \emph{pricing} did not transfer: the exchange
rate between attack and board cleanliness is set by the ruleset's award
table, and S2's all-spin and surge economics reprice exactly the lines
guideline play undervalues. The \emph{mechanisms} did not transfer:
stock has no value inside Attax's search --- no term prices a charged
back-to-back chain or a REN stock as an asset --- while the champion's
F14 layer is precisely such a term. And the \emph{world model} did not
transfer: death, garbage, and spin evidence were all approximated in
ways that were harmless or absent under guideline rules and decisive
under S2. Weight tuning sits downstream of all three, which is why two
honest campaigns could neither fix nor fairly measure the gap.

\textbf{The champion is the control case.} Sold Slear's own search is,
by the same standard, an approximation: it models incoming garbage as
scalar amounts only, with no hole geometry at all. The difference is not
fidelity but fit --- its approximations were selected, and its eleven
weight generations tuned, on this one referee. Its inherited engine is
the same Cold Clear 2 family Attax defeated 79--21 under guideline
rules; everything its author added is referee-specific adaptation.
Strength of this kind is real, and it is concentrated exactly where
transfer is weakest. We read the S2 gap as, in large part, an adaptation
gap --- which is a claim about where the remaining work is, not an
excuse for the scoreline.

\textbf{Protocols are doing epistemic work.} Every quantitative
statement in this paper that survived contact with an audit did so
because the protocol forced the evidence to exist: records before prose,
bars before runs, abort rules that fired when designed to, and an audit
trail that could convict our own probe of deviating from its
registration. The frozen-profile gate we ran against the Cold Clear 2
fork during development is the counterpart example from the guideline
side: its bar was missed, the run was not re-rolled, and its scoreline
appears nowhere in this paper --- a missed bar is data about a
configuration, not an achievement to be rounded up.

\section{7. Limitations}\label{limitations}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Replica, not executable.} Hoiko results are against a
  byte-faithful Rust/WASM replica validated on 900 moves and a full
  rules audit, not against the original executable, which the benchmark
  machine cannot run. Move-level agreement on three seeds is not a proof
  of equivalence over all games. Hoiko's wall-clock adaptation was
  disabled throughout.
\item
  \textbf{Scoped conditions.} The Cold Clear 2 and clys results hold at
  the stated time controls (100 ms windows, 5 PPS paired turns) and
  rules profiles. They are not equal-compute rankings: Attax performs
  fixed work per move while its opponents search against deadlines, and
  information differs by matchup (preview counts, board height visible,
  pending garbage unobserved over TBP).
\item
  \textbf{Descriptive intervals.} Wilson intervals for external matches
  are descriptive; seed pairs are the independent units.
\item
  \textbf{The S2 arena is an observation, not an authority.} The lab
  referee is a third-party re-implementation of the S2 ruleset. Defect
  findings are relative to that referee, and any future S2 result would
  inherit the caveat.
\item
  \textbf{Small campaigns.} Both S2 campaigns were short --- campaign 2
  completed 21 successful updates before its probe --- and their
  negative outcomes bound the designs tried, not the value of weight
  tuning under S2 in general. The probe's seat-layout deviation (Section
  5.2) is disclosed rather than repaired after the fact.
\item
  \textbf{Survival wins.} In two of four headline matchups Attax won
  while generating less attack per piece. The results establish game
  outcomes under the stated conditions, not superiority on every axis.
\end{itemize}
